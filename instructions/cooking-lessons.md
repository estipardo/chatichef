# Cooking lessons: what goes wrong and how to fix it

Upload it as a project document and keep adding whatever goes wrong for you. Claude should read it before suggesting one of these techniques. Spanish version: `cooking-lessons.es.md`.

## Cornflour creams (custard, flan, thickened sauces)

**The usual failure:** custard that is still runny after a whole night in the fridge.

**Why:** the sign "when it coats the back of the spoon" (~80 °C) is the one for **egg-only** custard. Cornflour doesn't thicken until it **starts to bubble**, close to 95 °C. Before that, the hot cream already seems to coat the spoon and fools you. And don't be afraid of it boiling: **cornflour protects the egg**, which is why pastry cream is boiled for a minute without splitting.

- **The right sign is bubbling**, and keeping it there for 1 minute while stirring. Second sign: when you drag the whisk along the bottom, you can see the bottom of the pan for a second.
- **With plant milk, 2 heaped teaspoons of cornflour per 200 ml**: almond milk is thinner and thickens less.
- In the microwave it's easy to fall short because you can't see the bubbling. For more than 2 servings, **use a saucepan**.
- **Rescue:** everything back into the pan, 1 more teaspoon of cornflour dissolved in cold milk, medium-low heat stirring until it bubbles + 1 minute. If there are lumps, 20 seconds in the blender.

## Spanish potato omelette

- **In a frying pan.** In the air fryer it comes out dry, without the juicy centre.
- Potato sliced 3 mm thick, fried over medium heat in plenty of oil (~250 ml in a 24 cm pan; it is strained and reused).
- **Drain it for 3–5 minutes in a colander**: it is what decides the calories most (~10 g of absorbed oil per person, between 70 and 110 kcal depending on how well it drains).
- Rest the hot potato in the beaten egg (that's what gives the juicy centre) and set it with 2 teaspoons of oil.
- A **24 cm pan with 6 eggs**: a bigger one comes out flat and dry.
- **Protein:** with 3 eggs per person it's ~19–22 g. To reach a high target, serve it with tuna or go up to 4 eggs.

## Homemade gluten-free or chickpea doughs (socca)

They break easily. If they've already failed for you, **buy the dough** and move on.

## Reheating a stew when there's sauce left but no meat

New fillets **browned in the same pan** (2 min per side, raw inside), take them out; sauce + 2–3 tablespoons of water into the pan, scraping the bottom until it bubbles; fillets back in for **4–5 min covered over low heat**. Never heat raw chicken together with cold sauce: it turns grey and dry.

## Dried brown lentils (pardina) from a packet

- 90 g dry per serving and 600 ml of water for two.
- **No soaking**: they are small and thin-skinned.
- 30–35 minutes. Skim during the first 2 minutes of hard boiling and then **low heat with the lid half on**: at a rolling boil they lose their skins.
- Salt at 20 minutes. If you need to add water, **hot**: cold water shrinks the skin.

## Expiry times that shape the menu

- **Thawed meat:** 24–48 h in the fridge and **it isn't refrozen raw**. You can cook it and freeze the dish. Throw it away if: sour smell, sticky surface, greyish colour.
- **Boiled cashews:** 3–4 days. The recipe that uses them goes at the start of the week. Throw them away if: sour or fermented smell, slimy surface, cloudy water.
- **Opened can of coconut milk:** 3–4 days **moved to a glass jar**, never in the can.
- **Cooked tomato sauce:** 4–5 days.
- **Opened smoked salmon:** 2–3 days.
- **Opened carton of stock:** 4–5 days. If there's some left, into the freezer in a glass jar with two fingers of headroom (3 months).
- **Fresh fish:** if the day might move, better buy it frozen from the start.
- **Sauces with cream (plant or dairy):** over low heat and without a rolling boil, or they split.
